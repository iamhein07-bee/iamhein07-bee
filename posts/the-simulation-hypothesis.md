Where did the word ‘simulation’ came from?

The word "Simulation" has deep roots in history, originally meaning "to pretend" or "to hide the truth."

Latin Origin: It comes from the Latin word simulatio (to imitate or copy) and simulare (to make like).



Original Meaning: For centuries, it meant deceit or hypocrisy. If you were "simulating," you were pretending to be something you weren't.



Scientific Shift: In the 1940s and 50s, with the invention of computers, the meaning changed. Scientists began using it to describe a mathematical model of a real-world process (like a flight simulator).



Modern Philosophy: In the late 20th century, philosophers like Jean Baudrillard used it to describe how our society is becoming more about symbols and images than the actual objects they represent.



Pop Culture: Films like The Matrix turned it into a household term, making people wonder if our entire reality is just high-tech "pretending."
