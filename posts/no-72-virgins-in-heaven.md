Islam

Concept: Some interpretations of the Quran and Hadith describe heavenly companions called Houris (or Hoor al-Ayn) for believing men in paradise. They are often depicted as beautiful celestial beings.



Interpretations: The idea of 72 Houris comes from some Hadith, not the Quran itself, and is a subject of debate. Some scholars interpret these beings literally, while others view them metaphorically as symbols of joy, purity, and spiritual companionship. It is also believed that women who enter paradise will be rewarded with their righteous earthly husbands, or receive an equally fulfilling spiritual reward.



Christianity

Concept: The dominant view is that there is no marriage or procreation in heaven.



Biblical Basis: Jesus's words in the Gospel of Matthew (22:30) are often cited: "For in the resurrection they neither marry nor are given in marriage, but are like angels in heaven." The focus of the afterlife is on a direct, spiritual relationship with God and the divine, not on earthly, physical relationships.



Hinduism

Concept: In some heavenly realms (Svarga), there are celestial beings known as Apsaras (divine, beautiful female beings).



Interpretation: These are temporary realms where one can enjoy the fruits of good karma before being reborn. The presence of Apsaras is a form of reward for a virtuous life, but it is not the ultimate goal. The final objective is to transcend all desires and attachments to achieve Moksha (liberation from the cycle of rebirth).



Buddhism

Concept: The ultimate goal is Nirvana—the liberation from all desires and attachments.



Interpretation: Rebirth in heavenly realms (devas) is possible for those with good karma, but these are still considered temporary states within the cycle of suffering (samsara). While male and female celestial beings exist in these realms, relationships and desires are considered attachments to be overcome on the path to enlightenment.



Judaism

Concept: There is no single, widely accepted doctrine on this. The afterlife (Olam Ha-Ba) is considered a spiritual realm.



Interpretation: Judaism focuses more on living a righteous and ethical life in this world rather than on specific physical rewards in the next. The belief in a physical or carnal reward of gaining women is not a central tenet.
